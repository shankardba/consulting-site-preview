Graph databases

Graph databases store things (dots) and the relationships between them (lines) as equals. When your questions sound like “who is connected to whom?”, nothing else comes close.

SpreadsheetsRelational databasesDocument databasesGraph databases

The example data is made up: a small neighbourhood bakery. Use the arrow keys or the dots to step through.

The short version

✓ Great for

  • Referral networks and social connections
  • Supply chains and product recalls
  • Fraud rings: shared phones, cards and addresses
  • Recommendations: “people like you also bought…”

✗ Watch out for

  • Totals, averages and monthly reports
  • Simple lists that don’t really have relationships
  • Smaller pool of people who know the tools
  • Usually an addition to your main database, not a replacement

When your questions are about how things connect, such as referrals, recalls, fraud rings and networks, use a graph.

Common tools

Neo4j, Amazon Neptune, Memgraph, and graph extensions for Postgres
