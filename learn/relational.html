Relational databases

Relational databases split data into tables that each hold one kind of thing, then link them with IDs. It’s been the backbone of business software for fifty years. Here’s why.

The example data is made up: a small neighbourhood bakery. Use the arrow keys or the dots to step through.

The short version

✓ Great for

  • Customers, orders, invoices, inventory, bookings
  • Many people and apps reading and writing at once
  • Money and anything else that must never be wrong (transactions)
  • Reports that combine several kinds of records (SQL joins)

✗ Watch out for

  • You design the structure first, and changing it later takes planning
  • Records that all look different don’t fit neat columns
  • Deep chains of relationships get slow (join after join)
  • Needs someone who knows SQL and database care

When the same kinds of records repeat and several people or apps share them, use a relational database.

Common tools

PostgreSQL, MySQL, SQL Server, Oracle, Amazon Aurora, SQLite

Over a decade with Oracle (certified), Postgres, SQL Server and Redshift. That includes automating care for 2,000+ databases and moving whole warehouses to the cloud.
